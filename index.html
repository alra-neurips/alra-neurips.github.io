When a dynamics shift changes how commanded actions translate into motion, the correction fits entirely in the action embedding. Every encoder and the predictor stay frozen. AdaJEPA's strongest variant updates 9,934,840 parameters at every adaptation step. ALRA updates 210 — one for every 47,309 — and recovers the same planning performance under a fiftyfold damping increase.

Method

A latent world model encodes an observation, a proprioceptive state, and an action, then predicts the next latent state. Under a dynamics shift the visual structure of the environment is unchanged; what changes is how a commanded action translates into motion. That is a miscalibration of the action channel, not of the representation.

ALRA computes a residual over the concatenated action and proprioceptive encodings through an affine map — a 10×20 weight matrix and a 10-dimensional bias, 210 parameters in total — and adds it to the action encoding before prediction. The map is zero-initialized, so the first plan of every episode is made by the unmodified pretrained model. It is optimized online against the same latent prediction term used during pre-training, over a replay buffer of the five most recent transitions, with one gradient step per replanning step.

Abstract

Latent world models degrade when deployed under unseen dynamics shifts. Recent test-time adaptation methods such as AdaJEPA address this by fine-tuning selected sub-networks of the predictor and visual encoder online, updating on the order of 107 parameters within every episode. We show that such updates are unnecessary when the shift acts on the agent's own dynamics: a 210-parameter affine residual applied to the action embedding, with all encoders and the predictor left entirely frozen, recovers as much planning performance as sub-network fine-tuning. On PointMaze-Medium, this residual matches the strongest AdaJEPA baseline under all three shifts — a fiftyfold damping increase, a fivefold mass reduction, and a tenfold mass increase — improving over the frozen baseline in each while leaving in-distribution performance unchanged, and updating four orders of magnitude fewer parameters. Our results suggest that dynamics shifts which rescale how commanded actions translate into motion can be corrected entirely in action space, leaving the learned visual and proprioceptive representation untouched.
