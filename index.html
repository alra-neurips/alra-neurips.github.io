Overview

Latent world models lose planning performance when the dynamics change at test time. AdaJEPA recovers some of it by fine-tuning sub-networks of the predictor and visual encoder, on the order of 107 parameters, in every episode. ALRA leaves the entire world model frozen and learns only a 210-parameter affine correction to the action embedding, online, from the same self-supervised latent prediction error.

On PointMaze-Medium, this matches the strongest AdaJEPA baseline under all three dynamics shifts (50× damping, 0.2× mass and 10× mass) and leaves in-distribution performance unchanged, suggesting that shifts which rescale how actions translate into motion can be corrected in action space alone.

Method

When an agent encounters a dynamics shift, the environment’s visual structure is unchanged; what changes is how a commanded action translates into motion. A shift that rescales this action-to-outcome mapping leaves the predictor’s visual model intact and manifests instead as a miscalibration of the action channel.

ALRA therefore keeps the visual, proprioceptive and action encoders and the predictor entirely frozen, and computes a residual from the concatenated action and proprioceptive embeddings with an affine map gω:

Δe(at, pt; ω) = gω([Eα(at) ; Eψ(pt)]) Ẽα(at, pt; ω) = Eα(at) + Δe(at, pt; ω)W ∈ ℝ10×20, b ∈ ℝ10: 210 parameters

The residual is zero-initialized, so adaptation begins exactly at the frozen model, and it is applied to each frame of the history window with weights shared across frames. Within each episode, only these 210 parameters are optimized online, using the latent prediction term of the pre-training objective over a replay buffer of the five most recent transitions, with one gradient step per replanning step. The residual is reset to zero at the start of every episode.

Adaptation across the episode

Under the 50× damping shift, ALRA’s benefit emerges earlier in the episode: by replanning step 12 it has recovered +19.2 points over the frozen baseline, where predfirst+enclast has recovered +5.0, with the two converging by step 20. We attribute this to the size of the adapted parameter set, but do not isolate it from the learning rate, and the effect is specific to this condition.

Scope and limitations

  1. Where it helps. ALRA’s effectiveness tracks how directly a shift rescales the mapping from action to motion. Under the 10× mass increase, which alters the agent’s momentum rather than the gain relating action to motion, no method, ALRA or the representational baselines, recovers more than a small share of the degradation.
  2. A single environment. All results are on PointMaze-Medium; whether the action-space decomposition holds under contact-rich dynamics, where a shift may act on a manipulated object rather than the agent, remains open.
  3. Learning rate per shift family. ALRA uses η = 10−2 for the control and damping conditions and 10−4 for density, whereas the baselines use a single learning rate from the original implementation. This gives ALRA a tuning advantage the baselines were not given.
  4. Per-episode recalibration. The residual is reset at the start of each episode and never persisted, so ALRA does not accumulate a correction across deployment.
